The gap this fills

Why brokerages that run trucks end up stitching two systems together

A pure brokerage TMS is built around one assumption: every load gets covered by an outside carrier. That works fine until a brokerage adds its own trucks, owner-operators, or a small asset fleet -- and suddenly dispatch, driver pay, and truck/trailer tracking don't fit anywhere in the software they already have. The usual fix is a second system bolted on next to the first: two logins, two places freight data lives, and a manual reconciliation step between them every time a load changes hands.

🚚

One dispatch board, both worlds

Owner operators, company trucks, and outside carriers all live on the same dispatch board -- covering a load with your own truck works exactly like covering it with a carrier.

💰

One set of books

Customer receivables, carrier payables, and driver pay all post to the same accounting module -- no exporting from one system and re-entering into another.

📍

One tracking view

Whether a load is tracked through carrier ELD integrations or your own in-house GPS tracking, it shows up the same way on the same tracking board.
